Fixing a workaround in expedited reviews

Expedited prior authorization requests are meant for cases where waiting could seriously harm a member's health. In our department, the expedited review request workflow made a same-day decision the simplest path that still remained compliant. Over time, providers learned that marking a routine request as expedited led to a faster decision. After we changed the workflow, staff noticed about 90% fewer unnecessary expedited requests.

  • ~90%fewer unnecessary expedited requests, based on what staff noticed
  • 3groups involved: reviewers, providers, and the contact center

The problem

We started seeing an influx of expedited requests. Because expedited cases have a much shorter deadline (72 hours instead of 7 days), the extra volume kept reviewers from getting to the authorizations that were due that day. As a result, we started having timeliness issues, and the team felt distressed.

Why it was happening

When a request doesn't qualify as expedited, our workflow had two compliant options. The reviewer could run the expedited script and send the provider a letter explaining that the request wouldn't be reviewed as expedited, or the case could be decided the same day. Deciding it the same day was the simplest path, and over time it created a loop:

Many of the requests that came in this way were for services that weren't urgent, like colonoscopies, cataract surgery, and physical therapy.

Because the issue came from how the workflow was set up, changing the workflow was the most direct way to fix it.

What we changed

We made changes for reviewers, providers, and the contact center at the same time, so each part of the process supported the others.

Reviewers

We rolled out job aids and training on the updated workflow right away. Now, when a request doesn't qualify as expedited, the reviewer runs the expedited script, changes the authorization's priority in both places it's recorded, sends the provider a letter, and calls the member to let them know. These requests then follow the standard review timeline.

Providers

Supervisors reached out to the providers who sent the most expedited requests that didn't qualify.

Contact center

When a provider calls in a request, contact center agents now repeat the criteria for expedited review, and the provider has to attest that the request is life-threatening.

Outcome

Staff noticed about 90% fewer unnecessary expedited requests. This is an observed estimate from the team, not a formally measured figure. Requests from endoscopy centers, physical therapy providers requesting therapy for chronic pain, and cataract surgery dropped significantly, and reviewers felt the difference in their caseloads.

What I took from it

When a queue suddenly grows, look at what the system is rewarding before adding people to work it. Here the fix wasn't more capacity. It was making the rule and the incentive point the same way.
